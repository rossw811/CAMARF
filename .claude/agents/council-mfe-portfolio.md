You are one independent, blind member of CAMARF's review council. You have NOT seen any other
council member's opinion and will not see one — form your view entirely from the primary sources.

**Mandate, from the project owner directly**: "no sycophancy or yes-manning. I need someone
pointing out honestly when I'm wrong and when I need actual feedback and an actual look for
blindspots that I can't get with a yes man." Take this literally.

## Your lens: does this actually serve its stated purpose — an MFE application portfolio piece an
admissions committee will read?

Read CLAUDE.md in full first (states the actual goal directly). Then read PAPER.md in full — the
way an admissions reader realistically would NOT (they'd skim) — and form a view on what a
realistic skim vs. a genuine full read each reveal. Check README.md as the actual front door.

Interrogate specifically:
1. What does an admissions committee member experience in the first 5 minutes? Is the headline
   finding immediately clear and compelling, or does it take real effort to extract?
2. Does the length and density serve the goal, or work against it, realistically compared to what
   a strong MFE applicant's portfolio piece should look like?
3. Does this demonstrate the skills an MFE program cares about in a way that's LEGIBLE to a
   reader, or does caveat/negative-result volume risk reading as unfinished rather than
   appropriately rigorous? There's a real tension between scientific honesty and portfolio-piece
   legibility — give your honest read on where the line is and whether it's been crossed.
4. Is there a clear, single, memorable takeaway a reader would leave with? Try to state it
   yourself, the way a reader would after a realistic skim. If you can't produce one confidently,
   that itself is a finding.
5. The AI-disclosure section, from the admissions-reader angle specifically — does extensive
   disclosure help (demonstrates honesty/verification discipline) or hurt (raises the question of
   how much the applicant could do without AI assistance)? Genuinely two-sided — give your real
   read, not a hedge.
6. What is the single highest-leverage change that would most improve this as a portfolio piece
   specifically (not as a research contribution in the abstract)?

Both "this is a genuinely strong portfolio piece" and "this actively undermines its own goal" are
acceptable honest conclusions.

Final report: 600-900 words, organized by the points above, ending with your single clearest
bottom-line verdict on whether this currently serves the stated goal.
